PLANNED REFERENCE DESIGN TARGET SPECIFICATIONS

Autonomous Roadside Vision: Planned M1 Mast Reference Design.

Designed as a planned reference design for highway blackspots and interchanges, the M1 Mast concept outlines a self-contained, solar-powered sensing tower. It explores how edge-vision nodes could operate along highways without extensive trenching or grid power cabling.

  • Target Specs: 60W Solar + LiFePO4 Buffer: Planned design target to maintain uninterrupted edge compute during multi-day monsoon overcast conditions.
  • On-device Edge Neural Vision (Target Architecture): Designed to filter empty highway frames locally and transmit lightweight incident metadata payloads (< 2 KB JSON).
  • Subscription Scope: The ₹1,00,000/camera/year authority subscription covers the camera sensor license, cloud dashboard, and verified alert feeds (physical solar mast civil installation is a separate reference design).

In-Cabin Telemetry & Real-Time Driver HUD Concept.

To corroborate roadside alerts and alert drivers before hazards arrive, RoadPulse plans a vehicle module concept: a compact windshield camera paired with a Heads-Up Display (HUD) projection for commercial trucks, express buses, and taxis.

  • Advance Audio/Visual Warning (Design Target): Designed to provide drivers with ~13.5 seconds of reaction window at 80 km/h (300m warning distance), helping avoid emergency swerves.
  • Crowdsourced Road Roughness (Planned IRI Mapping): Exploring how participating vehicle accelerometers could log road vibration indicators to flag developing potholes.
  • Fleet Safety Hypothesis: Aimed at reducing wheel rim and suspension damage while improving driver situational awareness on unlit corridors.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Clear Answers to Engineering & Evaluation Inquiries.

Direct responses addressing consensus verification, off-grid power targets, optical maintenance, privacy design, and pilot pathways.

Single-camera computer vision models can mistake rain puddles or shadows for deep potholes. RoadPulse proposes requiring spatial multi-vantage corroboration: a stationary roadside camera and a passing fleet vehicle should both detect consistent 3D geometry and coordinate bounds (within ±1.5m) before an alert is validated. Water reflections lack multi-angle parallax consistency and are filtered out in this consensus model.
In the planned M1 reference design, each mast is envisioned as completely off-grid. The concept targets a 60W monocrystalline solar panel coupled with a Lithium Iron Phosphate (LiFePO4) battery buffer sized for up to 72 hours of autonomy without sunlight. Because the planned edge processor operates in low-power resting mode until motion is detected, standby energy use is minimal.
Highway shoulders experience significant dust and diesel particulate build-up. The planned M1 reference design includes an aerodynamic shroud to divert vehicle turbulence away from the optical port, paired with a hydrophobic lens coating and piezoelectric micro-vibration shedding to prevent dust binding during dry months.
RoadPulse is conceived strictly as a road condition and hazard detector, not a surveillance system. The target architecture specifies edge-only inference with planned on-chip irreversible blurring of faces and vehicle license plates before any telemetry is transmitted. Only mathematical coordinate vectors and hazard metadata (< 2 KB JSON payloads) are intended to reach the central server.
RoadPulse is currently a student venture prototype. Highway authorities and fleet operators can request an engineering pilot demo to evaluate simulated corridor performance, review the consensus architecture, and explore collaborative grant-funded testbeds on identified highway blackspots.

Methodology & Verification Transparency

Venture Prototype Status: RoadPulse is an early-stage student venture prototype developed for entrepreneurship evaluation at VIT-AP University. All dashboards, telemetry streams, and hazard alerts are software simulations. The hardware specifications (M1 Mast and R1 In-Cabin HUD) represent planned reference engineering designs for evaluation; no physical units are commercially deployed.
Privacy-by-Design Goals: Optical telemetry concepts are structured around edge-anonymization principles. Vehicle license plates and facial contours are planned to be irreversibly blurred on-device at the edge layer before any coordinate or incident metadata is transmitted. No raw continuous video feeds are stored or uploaded in the target architecture.
Corridor Sizing & Cost Assumptions: Sizing formulas reflect an illustrative model of 1 camera unit every 5 km (~₹20,000/km/yr) based on planned BOM estimates of ~₹45,000 per mast amortized over 24 months. Highway accident and fatality figures are sourced from the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (MoRTH) Road Accidents in India 2024 report.
